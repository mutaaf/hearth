Privacy policy

Your TVs are your business.

Hearth talks to your TVs over your own Wi-Fi. There's no account, no sign-in and no cloud in between. Here is everything that does and doesn't leave your phone.

What stays on your phone

  • Your TVs: their names, rooms, addresses on your network, models and the pairing keys they gave Hearth.
  • Your scenes, schedules and settings.
  • What's on each TV, its volume, and everything you press.

None of this is sent to us or anyone else, unless you choose to share your house (below), and then only locked so that we can't read it. Hearth reaches your TVs directly on your local network, which is why iOS asks for Local Network access.

Sharing your house (optional)

Settings → Share this house hands your TVs, their pairing keys and your scenes to a family member's phone, so they don't have to pair every TV again. Your phone locks all of it first (XChaCha20-Poly1305 encryption) with a new random key made for that one invite. Only the locked copy goes to Hearth's server; the key exists only in the invite link, after the #, which browsers and apps never send to a server. We can't open the copy and never see the key. The copy is deleted the moment the other phone fetches it, or after 24 hours if nobody does, and it isn't logged or backed up. Anyone who gets the link before it's used can open it, so send it only to people you'd hand your TV remotes to. Joining works the same way in reverse: the invite is unlocked on the phone that joins.

Anonymous usage and crash reports

To find bugs and see which features earn their keep, the app sends anonymous events to PostHog, an analytics service:

  • that the app opened, and how many TVs it has;
  • for each button press or app launch: the kind of action (say, "volume"), the TV's brand, how long the TV took and whether it worked;
  • reconnects, Lock Screen presses, the Hearth Pro screen and purchase outcome (bought, cancelled or failed), and Light Racer opening;
  • error reports, with TV names, network addresses and serial numbers removed on the phone before sending.

From version 1.1, the app also sends a few usage counts to mutaafaziz.com's own analytics relay (Box Office), the same one this website uses: that a session started, which screen is open (say, "remote" or "settings"), the name of a remote button pressed (say, "up" or "volume"), and that pairing a TV started or finished, with the TV's brand. Only those names and numbers, the app's version and a random install ID go; never a TV's name, its address or anything you type. The relay removes cookies and shortens your IP address before passing them to PostHog, and no location is looked up. These counts are linked to that install ID, so we can tell a returning phone from a new one.

Each install's random IDs are made on the phone and aren't tied to you, your Apple ID or your device's advertising identifier; deleting the app removes them. We don't build profiles, don't look up location from IP addresses, and ask PostHog to discard them. We never sell or share this data, and it is never used for advertising or tracking across apps.

Settings → Share usage stats turns all of this off (the usage counts, the anonymous events and the crash reports) and deletes the install IDs and anything waiting to be sent.

Connection help (optional)

When you ask how to connect a TV, or ask the AI about it, Hearth sends that TV's brand, model code and software version, and what went wrong if something did, to Hearth's server, where an AI writes the steps for that model. The answer is kept and shared with anyone who has the same model, so it's only about the TV, never about you.

If you tap Send it to us, Hearth sends the same, plus which connection checks passed or failed, a short error message and the note you type, with network addresses, serial numbers and long numbers taken out on your phone first. You see the list before anything is sent. We keep these reports to add support for TVs that don't connect yet; each gets a ticket number so the app can tell you when yours is fixed. They're never tied to you, your Apple ID or your device.

Purchases

Hearth Pro is bought through Apple. Apple handles payment; we never see your card or Apple ID. The app only learns from Apple whether Pro is unlocked on this Apple ID or Family Sharing group.

"What your TVs cost" (optional)

If you choose to read a power bill, Hearth reads it on your phone and sends only the lines with charges and rates (never your name, address or account number) to Hearth's server, which works out your price per kWh. It isn't stored. You see exactly what will be sent first.

Ads

The Hearth app and the Hearth on your home network never show ads. Only the public demo on this website (with pretend TVs) shows a small ad below it; see the ad privacy policy.

This website

The Hearth pages on mutaafaziz.com (this site, the public demo and Light Racer) collect anonymous usage analytics: which pages are viewed, clicks and demo button presses, approximate city-level location, and session replays with everything typed masked. It goes to PostHog through mutaafaziz.com, with your IP address shortened before it leaves and no cookies. If your browser sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track, nothing is collected. The Hearth app and the Hearth on your home network never load it (the app's own usage counts, above, go through the same relay), and it never runs on the kids area of mutaafaziz.com. The page that opens a house invite (/hearth/join) doesn't load it either.

Children

Hearth collects nothing that identifies anyone, of any age.

Your choices

  • Turn off Settings → Share usage stats in the app: nothing more is sent, and its install IDs are deleted.
  • Delete the app, and everything it kept on your phone goes with it.
  • Questions or a request about your data: open an issue and we'll answer.
