Setup

So easy a caveman could do it.

Three steps. No app store, no account, no IP addresses, no codes.

  1. 1

    Turn your TVs on.

    Make sure your phone is on the same Wi-Fi. Already added a TV that’s off? Hearth can wake it for you.

  2. 2

    Tap Find my TVs.

    Hearth listens on your network and every LG, Samsung, Roku and Google TV answers. Names, models, all of it.

  3. 3

    Say yes once, if it asks.

    Roku and Google TV just connect. So do 2016–17 Samsungs. Newer Samsungs ask for Allow once. An LG shows a number you type into your phone, so a TV mounted up high is fine.

  4. ✓

    That’s it. Sit down.

    Swipe to move, hold to repeat, one tap to turn the whole house off at bedtime.

What you get

A remote that feels like a remote.

Swipe or tap the pad

Use it like a touchpad or press the arrows. Every press clicks back with a tiny buzz (on iPhone it lands as your finger lifts).

Hold to repeat

Hold volume or an arrow and it keeps going, faster the longer you hold.

Your apps, one tap

Netflix, YouTube, whatever the TV or Roku has, on a shelf under your thumb.

Works with

LG webOS · 2014+

  • Connect: a number on screen, or Allow
  • Pad, back, home, media keys
  • Volume with live level
  • Off, and on via Wake-on-LAN
  • App shelf with icons
  • Named HDMI inputs
  • Messages on screen

Samsung Tizen · 2016+

  • Connect: Allow once (2016–17: nothing)
  • Pad, back, home, media keys
  • Volume up/down (no level)
  • Off, and on via Wake-on-LAN
  • App shelf
  • Source button (no named inputs)
  • Messages on screen

Roku players & TVs

  • Connect: nothing to press
  • Pad and keys, with real press-and-hold
  • Volume up/down (no level)
  • Off, and on from standby (sticks can’t wake from Wi-Fi)
  • App shelf with icons
  • Inputs (Roku TVs only)
  • Messages on screen

Google TV + Android TV

  • Connect: nothing to press, no code
  • Volume with live level, mute
  • Apps: YouTube, Netflix, Spotify, Plex
  • See what’s on (apps opened by cast)
  • Arrow keys (need Google’s one-time code)
  • Power (needs Google’s one-time code)
  • Messages on screen

Install

One command on a computer that stays on.

A Mac mini, an old laptop, a Raspberry Pi. You need Node.js 20.19+. Run this, then scan the QR code it prints with your phone.

~/
$ git clone https://github.com/mutaaf/hearth
$ cd hearth && npm run up
✓ Node 22.12 ✓ Dependencies installed 🔥 Hearth is ready · scan the QR with your phone

Questions

Good questions.

Why doesn’t it just run on this website?

On purpose. Browsers, rightly, won’t let a website reach the devices on your home network. Hearth runs inside your house, so your TVs, pairing keys and viewing habits never leave it. The demo on this page uses pretend TVs.

Will it work with my TV?

If it’s an LG smart TV from 2014 on (webOS), a Samsung smart TV from 2016 on (Tizen), any Roku player or Roku TV, or a Google TV / Android TV, almost certainly. Google TVs get volume, mute and apps without any code; arrow keys there need Google’s one-time code, which is coming as an optional unlock. Vizio, Sony and Apple TV are on the roadmap.

Do I need codes or accounts?

No accounts, ever. Roku and Google TV connect with nothing to press. Samsungs from 2016–17 too; newer ones ask for Allow once. An LG either shows a number you type into your phone (no reaching for the TV’s remote) or asks for Allow.

Can it break my TV?

No. Hearth only uses the network remote control each TV already ships with, the same one the brand’s own phone app uses. It doesn’t install anything on the TV or change its software.

Can it turn a TV on?

Yes, with Wake-on-LAN, once you switch on “Turn on via Wi-Fi” (LG) or “Power On with Mobile” (Samsung) in the TV’s settings. It works even for a TV you haven’t connected yet, and setup wakes an off TV for you. Rokus turn on from standby; a stick that’s fully unpowered can’t be woken over Wi-Fi. Google TVs need Google’s code for power, so not yet.

My TVs are slow and full of ads. Can it fix that?

Hearth makes controlling them fast. To make the TVs themselves fast, follow the tune-up guide: about 20 minutes per TV covers settings, whole-house ad blocking, a proper reset, LG homebrew, or a cheap box that turns the TV into a plain screen.

What does it cost?

Nothing. It’s open source under the MIT license, with no accounts, subscriptions or ads.
